## The situation

Ceibo Dental is a fictional two-chair family practice in Washington Heights. Picture an office like many in the neighborhood: most patients prefer Spanish, nearly everyone finds the practice on their phone, and the front desk spends a good part of the day answering the same three questions. Are you open Saturday? Do you take my insurance? How much is a cleaning?

The typical website for an office like this was built in English and has a translate widget bolted on top. The Spanish reads like a machine wrote it, because one did. Booking means calling, and the insurance list lives in a PDF.

## What we changed

**Spanish first, not Spanish second.** The site opens in Spanish for visitors whose phone is set to Spanish, and remembers the choice. English is one tap away in the header, never hidden in a footer. Both versions are written, not translated, so they sound like the people at the front desk.

**Answer the three questions on the first screen.** Hours, accepted insurance and starting prices sit right under the headline. If a patient only reads one screen, they still get what they came for.

**Booking without a phone call.** Three steps: pick the reason for the visit, pick a time, leave a name and phone number. The confirmation arrives by text or WhatsApp, whichever the patient chooses. Nothing about health history is collected here, that stays in the office's own system.

**Plain language.** We wrote at an everyday reading level in both languages. "Limpieza" instead of "profilaxis". "Root canal" with a one-line explanation of what it actually is.

## Design decisions worth calling out

- The language switch says "Español / English", never flags. A flag picks a country, and patients come from a dozen of them.
- Prices are shown as "starting at" ranges. For patients without insurance, not knowing the price is often the reason they never call.
- Big tap targets and short pages. Many patients use older phones on slow connections, so the site loads fast and works without fancy effects.
- The WhatsApp button goes to a business number the office controls, with a pre-filled message so the patient doesn't have to start from a blank screen.
